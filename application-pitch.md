## Application pitch

### Relay

**Motivation.** Student-club handoffs can fail in two opposite ways: useful knowledge disappears when an officer leaves, or outdated knowledge keeps being passed down after the situation behind it has changed.

**Relay** is a lightweight shared playbook that helps student organizations preserve recurring processes without turning handoff into another administrative burden.

**Quick Playbooks.** Officers can record recurring processes such as competition registration, reimbursements, or storage renewal as short, ordered steps. Officers control the official playbook so the club always has a clear source of truth. Club members can read these processes and attach a question to a specific step or note when something is unclear, giving officers a simple way to see where future handoffs may still be confusing. Incoming officers therefore begin with what the club already knows instead of reconstructing the process from old files or former officers.

**Context Notes.** When a process has an important explanation, officers can add a short optional note about it. A note might capture an outside rule, a decision the club made, or something learned from experience. Relay does not require an explanation for every step; officers add context only when losing it would make the process harder to understand later. If the same context matters to several playbooks, the existing note can be reused instead of written repeatedly.

**Change Check.** When an officer learns that shared context has changed, they update it once. Relay then surfaces the playbooks that relied on it so officers can decide whether those procedures still make sense. Relay does not monitor outside policies or decide what is correct; it makes the consequences of a known change visible before an outdated assumption is passed to another group of officers.

Relay stays close to how clubs already work: officers write down what they know, members ask questions when something is unclear, and future officers inherit that knowledge. The difference is that the steps, the important context behind them, and the need to reconsider them remain connected across leadership changes.
