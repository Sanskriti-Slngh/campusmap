## User Journey

**1. Tiya inherits instructions that look trustworthy.** Tiya has just taken over rehearsal-space logistics for the MIT Dance Team after the previous officer graduated. In Relay, she opens the **Book Rehearsal Space** Playbook shown in **Fig. 2, Playbook detail**, and sees a short ordered process instead of having to reconstruct it from old emails. One Step says to check that the rehearsal-space renewal is current before the semester begins. Attached to that Step is the Context Note explaining the assumption behind it: **MIT event spaces only need to be renewed once per academic year**. Because that explanation is preserved with the Playbook, Tiya can see not just what the club has been doing, but what that Step depended on.

**2. The procedure survives, but one of its assumptions stops being true.** A few months later, Tiya learns from the space office that renewals are now required **each semester**. The Playbook itself has not disappeared; in fact, that is what makes the situation dangerous. If Tiya simply left the inherited Playbook alone, the old annual-renewal assumption could remain in the club's documentation and look authoritative to the next officer. As shown in **Fig. 3, Changing a Context Note**, Tiya updates the shared note and chooses **Save as changed information** rather than **Save as correction**, because the meaning—not just the wording—has changed.

**3. One change exposes every procedure that relied on the old assumption.** That Context Note is also used by Competition Registration and the Officer Transition Checklist. Tiya does not have to remember where the old rule was copied or search through several documents. After she records the change once, **Fig. 1, Club workspace / Playbooks**, shows that three Playbooks need review and highlights Book Rehearsal Space as one of them. Because Relay stores the changed rule once and tracks which Playbooks rely on it, Tiya updates one fact instead of searching through three separate procedures for copies of the old assumption.

**4. Tiya reviews the procedure instead of letting Relay guess.** In **Fig. 4, Reviewing an affected Playbook**, Tiya sees the old and new Context Note next to the affected Step: **"Check that the rehearsal-space renewal is current before the semester begins."** The Step may already be broad enough to remain correct, so Relay does not rewrite it automatically. Tiya decides whether to confirm it as current or mark that changes are needed. When the review is resolved, the Playbook has been deliberately checked against the new rule rather than simply carried forward.

When Tiya eventually hands the role to the next officer, they inherit more than a list of steps. They inherit the updated semester-renewal assumption and a Playbook that has already been reviewed against it. Relay therefore addresses both ways handoffs fail: important knowledge is less likely to disappear, and outdated knowledge is less likely to survive simply because no one remembers why it was written that way.
